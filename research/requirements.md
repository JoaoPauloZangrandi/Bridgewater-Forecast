# Forecasting the Future 2026: frozen requirements

Information cutoff: 2026-07-30, America/Sao_Paulo.

## Official deliverable

- One PDF uploaded through the participant's personalized submission link.
- Part 1: at least 10 binary forecasts, one sentence each, each with a
  probability and clear, objective resolution criteria.
- Forecast horizons: short and medium term, from one to five years.
- Scope: Modern Mercantilism, artificial intelligence, or their intersection.
- Part 2: a coherent framework and holistic synthesis, no more than three pages.
- Part 3: an analytical appendix, recommended at five pages or less.
- Practical total: aim for ten pages or less because reviewers may stop there.
- Deadline: 2026-08-01 at 23:59 US Eastern Time.

## Evaluation

- Forecasts: calibration and resolution.
- Framework and analysis: clarity, originality, and rigor.
- The framework must explain cause-and-effect dynamics and reference the most
  important forecasts rather than repeat each forecast's justification.

## Eligibility and authorship constraint

The submission is individual. The entrant represents that its substantive
elements are independently conceived original work and are not substantially
AI-generated. AI tools are allowed, but generic LLM output is explicitly
disfavored. The participant therefore must personally choose, challenge, and
own the thesis, forecast wording, probabilities, and final prose before
submission.

## Page architecture

1. Part 1: F01-F06 and the portfolio claim.
2. Part 1: F07-F12 and four empirical anchors.
3. Part 2: framework and causal mechanism.
4. Part 2: state behavior, capital flows, and bottlenecks.
5. Part 2: winners, losers, scenarios, and falsification.
6. Part 3: method, calibration, and cross-forecast consistency.
7. Part 3: AI capability, diffusion, productivity, capital, and energy.
8. Part 3: U.S. chips, EU compute, and mineral refining.
9. Part 3: China, MFN trade, tariffs, and multilateral institutions.
10. Resolution registry and selected sources.

## Quality gates

- Every event is binary and cannot be "mostly true."
- Every threshold has a named source, release vintage, time window, and rule for
  revisions or discontinued series.
- No event resolves at the information cutoff; persistence events retain a
  genuine path to reversal before their future resolution date.
- Forecast probabilities are not clustered around 50-70%.
- Correlated forecasts are identified and jointly stress-tested.
- Facts are distinguished from judgment and from conditional scenarios.
- The PDF has no unresolved references, clipped text, missing glyphs, overflow,
  broken links, or pages beyond ten.
- The participant completes an authorship review before the PDF is designated
  final.
